Former President Donald Trump was found guilty on all 34 counts of falsifying business records on Thursday afternoon. The convictions could bring him a prison sentence of four years. It is the first time that a former US president has been tried or convicted in a criminal case. As Trump left the courthouse, he again claimed that the trial was “rigged”. “This was a rigged, disgraceful trial. The real verdict will be decided by the people on November 5,” Trump said. In Connecticut, reactions were received from voters and legal experts. Some voters agreed that the ruling was unfair. “The judge was completely against him. Our democracy is ruined with this man in the White House,” said Patrick McGuire of New Milford. Others said the verdict was justified. “Great. It's long overdue,” said Susan from New Milford. Prosecutors said the evidence was clear. “They reviewed call logs, text messages and emails. They listened to recordings. They saw checks and bills,” said Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg. Chris Mattei is a former federal prosecutor who put Gov. John Rowland in prison. He said the jury found not only that Trump falsified business records, but also that he did so to influence the 2016 election. “They concluded that Donald Trump did indeed falsify his business records or have them falsified. And the reason he did that was because he wanted to violate campaign finance laws,” Mattei said. It involves the payment of “hush money” totaling $130,000 to porn star Stormy Daniels in exchange for her silence about an alleged sexual experience with Trump in 2006. Mattei said he was confident the former president would appeal the charges and remain free during the appeal process. “The question is whether the sentence could be carried out during his presidency if he continues to be a candidate for President of the United States and wins. Of course, I think the answer is probably 'no'. In that case, the sentence would be suspended until he leaves office,” Mattei said. There are three more trials pending against Trump, but they are unlikely to begin before this year's presidential election in November. Republican House Leader Vincent Candelora and Republican Senate Leader Stephen Harding also responded to the ruling with the following statement: “As lawyers, we have the utmost respect for the American justice system and share an unwavering belief in the rule of law. As legislators, we do everything we can to support the work of our judges, juries, and prosecutors in preserving the most basic institutions of our republic. But we cannot ignore the fact that the events in New York City suggest that those same institutions are being weaponized to achieve a political goal. These events have created a deeply troubling scenario that will inevitably end up on appeal and raise serious constitutional challenges. We are only certain that our Constitution will prevail.” Connecticut State Party Chair Nancy DiNardo also issued the following statement on the ruling: “Today's ruling is gratifying and sends a strong message that no one is above the law. We will work every day through November 5 to ensure that Connecticut voters deliver another verdict that Donald Trump is deeply unfit to serve as president and that Joe Biden is the leader we need in the White House.” The sentencing of Trump is scheduled for July 11, just days before his expected official nomination as the Republican presidential candidate.
