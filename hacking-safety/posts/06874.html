Mouth and taste problems

www.cancer.org.nz · https://www.cancer.org.nz/cancer/cancer-treatment/side-effects-of-cancer-and-treatment/mouth-and-taste-problems/

Problems with your mouth, taste and eating can impact your daily life and enjoyment of food. Cancer and its treatment can change the way your salivary glands work and affect your sense of taste, texture and smell. Your oral health is very important to your overall health, especially as you go through cancer treatment. Some food may have less taste during cancer treatment than before, and others may have a salty, bitter or metallic taste. Your mouth may be more sensitive to food and drinks that are cold, hot, spicy or fizzy. These changes can affect your enjoyment of food. These ideas may help to improve the taste of your food: Here are some ways to help reduce off-putting food smells: Some cancer treatments, such as chemotherapy and radiation to the head and neck, can affect the salivary glands, which produce saliva also called spit. This can cause a lack of saliva, or sometimes thick saliva or mucous which makes eating difficult. A dry mouth can occur due to dehydration. A dry mouth and lack of saliva significantly increase your risk of tooth decay and infections in the mouth and throat. Here are some things that can help: Before you start treatment, you should have a dental check-up to make sure there is no mouth infection that can complicate cancer treatment. Treatment to your mouth and throat, a lack of saliva (dry mouth), and the use of extra snacks or drinks can affect your oral health. Take extra care of your teeth to reduce risks of infection and dental problems by: Mouth sores, ulcers (mucositis), infections, and swallowing difficulties (dysphagia) can occur due to treatment for your cancer. Poor teeth or ill-fitting dentures can also cause pain, making chewing and swallowing difficulties worse. If you need to adjust the texture of your food, try soft, pureed or minced food. If you have ongoing issues with mouth or swallowing, you may need a referral to a dietitian or speech-language therapist.
