No clear winner emerges after iowa democratic caucuses

www.abc.net.au · https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-02-07/no-clear-winner-emerges-after-iowa-democratic-caucuses/11944380

1 of 27 sentences flagged

US Election 2020 Iowa caucuses results are in but chaos and confusion reign as no clear winner emerges The winner of the first Democratic presidential primary in Iowa remains unclear, despite 100 per cent of voting precincts reporting their results. Former South Bend, Indiana, mayor Pete Buttigieg leads Vermont senator Bernie Sanders by two state delegate equivalents out of 2,152 counted — a margin of 0.09 percentage points — following the Iowa Democratic Party's release of the results from all 1,765 precincts in the state's Democratic caucuses. But both candidates have declared themselves victorious and there is evidence the party has not accurately tabulated some of its results. The Associated Press said it was unable to declare a winner. The New York Times has reported the contest was a "dead heat". The state party apologised for technical glitches with an app that slowed down reporting of results from the caucuses and spent the week trying to verify results. Before the final counts were reported, Democratic National Committee (DNC) chairman Tom Perez called for a "recanvass" of the results to "assure public confidence" after three days of technical issues and delays. That process would likely require state officials to review caucus math worksheets completed at more than 1,600 caucus sites to ensure the calculations were done correctly and matched the reported results. Instead of getting a Democratic primary winner in Iowa, the country was left with confusion, anger and still no declaration of an official victor. Iowa chairman Troy Price suggested in a statement that he would only pursue a recanvass if one was requested by a campaign. The New York Times reported that if a recanvassing was to go ahead, the result of the caucus may not be known before the New Hampshire primary is held on Tuesday (local time). Senator Sanders said he was planning to move on to the next primary contest when questioned whether he would request a recanvass. "I suspect that at the end of the day, Mr Buttigieg and I will have an equal number of delegates to the national convention," he said. The caucus crisis has been an embarrassing twist after months of promoting Iowa as a chance for Democrats to find some clarity in a jumbled 2020 field. He's young, he's gay and he's the mayor of a town the size of Ballarat, but don't rule out Pete Buttigieg just yet, writes Emily Olson. Instead, after a build-up that featured seven rounds of debates, nearly $US1 billion ($1.49 billion) spent nationwide and a year of political jockeying, caucus day ended with no winner and no official results. Campaigning in New Hampshire, Senator Sanders called the Iowa Democratic Party's management of the caucuses a "screw-up" that has been "extremely unfair" to the candidates and their supporters. The chaos surrounding the reporting breakdown seems sure to blunt the impact of Iowa's election, which typically rewards winners with a surge of momentum heading into subsequent primary contests. Mr Buttigieg and Senator Sanders will emerge from Iowa's caucuses with the most delegates to the party's national convention, regardless of which one eventually wins the contest. They have each won at least 11 national delegates, with a handful of delegates still to be awarded, according to the AP delegate count. Massachusetts senator Elizabeth Warren has won at least five delegates, while former vice-president Joe Biden has won at least two and Minnesota Senator Amy Klobuchar has at least one. Iowa will award 41 pledged delegates to the DNC national convention in July, where the winning candidate will be announced.
