A left-wing Labour party might consider repealing government guidance that requires schools to teach children that there are only two biological sexes, but given the Conservative government’s failure to turn the policy into law this guidance could easily be overturned. Shadow Education Secretary Bridget Phillipson Said If Labour is elected, as is widely expected, it will review guidance from Rishi Sunak’s government instructing educators to refrain from teaching far-left gender ideology as fact. This includes theories that people are born in the wrong body, that people change their gender identity or live in new gender grey zones such as “non-binary”. The guidance also instructs teachers to make it clear to students that there are only two genders: male and female. But given that the Conservatives have refused to codify such a position into law, leaving it only in technically voluntary guidance, there will be few obstacles for the next government to issue new guidance. Mr Phillipson was asked multiple times by the BBC on Sunday whether a Labour government would reverse the guidance and refused to give a definitive answer, but suggested changes were possible. “Many aspects of the draft (guidelines) contained good, easy-to-understand principles, but I think other elements are too partisan and loaded with unnecessary language, making it difficult for schools to comply,” she said, adding that Labour would “consult” on the issue before making a final decision.
