Bmcagainstprivacy mumbais now forcing women to reveal partners name before abortion 244159

Gynecologists across the city have boycotted a new BMC form for reporting medical termination of pregnancies because it seeks, apart from other personal information, the identity of the patient's partner. While the Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act mandates that the doctors should not even reveal the patient's name, the new BMC form demands from them such intimate details like residential address, contact number, details of contraceptive used if any, and reasons why it may have not worked. Representational image Doctors have refused to fill the online form as it not only goes against the doctorpatient confidentiality pact, but also violates the Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, exposing them to legal consequences. Criticising the BMC's cavalier attitude towards patients' privacy, Dr Arun Nayak, president, Mumbai chapter of the Federation of Obstetric and Gynaecological Societies of India, said doctors reveal details of a medical termination of pregnancy only if a court of law so demands in a medicolegal case. "Otherwise, we are not supposed reveal any details of the patient, forget her partner's," he said. Dr Jaydeep Tank, who heads the MTP committee of the Federation of Obstetric and Gynaecological Societies of India, said the BMC should have consulted doctors before framing the new reporting protocol. "We are bound by the law. I am sure the BMC will have to revise the forms. Majority of MTP patients are unmarried. Imagine the consequences of the data the BMC is seeking falling in the wrong hands," he said. The new reporting form is part of the civic body's efforts to crackdown on female foeticide in the wake of the state and the city recording a dismal female sex ratio. Maharashtra had recorded just 894 females per 1,000 males in the 2011 census. The city was the worst performer in Maharashtra with 838 females for every 1,000 males. Ever since, the civic body has been monitoring abortion clinics, and sonography centres. Till now, doctors were required to report only the number of abortions conducted at their clinics and the religious and economic background of the patients. The doctors first learnt about the new, online reporting system when they were called for a training session organised by the BMC's F South Ward last month. Dr Mohan Gadam, president of an association of gynaecologists and obstetricians, said the new forms bring doctors in direct conflict with their patients. "Patients can sue us for revealing their identities. They can ask for compensation and our licences can be revoked by the Maharashtra Medical Council for compromising a patient's privacy," he said. The city has seen a steady rise in the number of abortions in recent years, according to the BMC's data. The city recorded 27,256 abortions in 2012-13 against 16,977 in 2010-11. BMC's Deputy Executive Health Officer Dr Mangala Gomare said that she agrees with the doctors' concerns. "In MTP cases, a patient's privacy is paramount," she said, adding that necessary changes in the online reporting of MTP cases have already been ordered.
