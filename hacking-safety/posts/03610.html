Failing better and falling with style. Thanks for the reply!I feel like the current draft is a rewrite. Yes, with huge chunks cut, rearranged, etc. That's partly why it's been over a year in doing it. I seem to already follow most of the agreed-on advice (which I find encouraging), and I know there's another rewrite to come (because I looked back at the early pages of this one and thought "who wrote this crap? ").I can't be a tweaker: as I said in the original email, going back and redoing the last thousand words was a habit that had been preventing me finishing stuff, because I would just redo the same passages again and again instead of doing anything new. Since I sent this question, I have found a balance: I'm writing a story in instalments purely for fun, for my sister, which gives me motivation to do some work on it every day (since I know I have an eager audience waiting) while still keeping the main focus on the rewrite. I already know there are things I need to change again on the second rewrite (like, chunks I want to cut or rearrange) but the principle for me remains: get to the end, then go back. Thanks again for the advice! Yay. I'm glad you've found your own way that works. For the longest time, I misunderstood how rewriting worked. I believed that if I rewrote my novel, I would just create a new set of mistakes, and I would never finish editing my book to satisfaction! But recently, I realized, like you mentioned here, that it's easier to get yourself to do deep structural changes if you rewrite, rather than just doing edits on your first draft. My story especially needs rewriting because, over time, due to some personal life changes, my writing tone and style have drastically changed. I felt stuck for so long on what to do, because my story became this huge mess of inconsistent writing. But much later, I realized I could fix this problem by rewriting the entire work in my new writing style! Yes, I was concerned about how to do the revisions properly without falling into the trap of editing it forever, and never getting it out there. So I was very glad to look at some different ideas on how many revisions to do, when to take breaks, etc. Every author is indeed different, but it was helpful to read about how you and other authors approach this!
