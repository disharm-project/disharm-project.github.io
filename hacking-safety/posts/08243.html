It has a long and narrow blade, distinguishing it from the wide-bladed sabers commonly used in China. The blade features a triangular shape near the tip and a curved shape near the handle, with decorative hooks on the back of the blade resembling the shape of flying geese, from which it gets its name. The Yanling Dao has a long history of use in China, particularly during the Ming and Qing dynasties. It can be used for chopping, cleaving, and thrusting, making it a versatile close-combat weapon.
