DENVER — It’s been a good year so far for Sean Behrens, and the 21-year-old defenseman looks to continue that success with the Colorado Avalanche this fall. In April, Behrens helped the University of Denver win a record-tying 10th NCAA men’s hockey national championship, signed a three-year entry-level contract with the Avalanche and began his professional career with Colorado in the American Hockey League. Now he’s using those accomplishments to stand out among other prospects ahead of rookie camp and training camp. “I think I’m in a good spot here with the Avs,” Behrens said at Colorado’s development camp earlier this month. “I’m really excited to get started, whether it’s in Loveland (where the AHL affiliate plays) or Denver. I’m excited to play games, join the organization, play professional hockey and really start my hockey career. “So it’s kind of starting now, but it’s just getting as much as I can from different guys through practice, games, development camps. I’m really excited.” Despite only playing in two games in the AHL last season (one during the Calder Cup Playoffs), Behrens, a second-round pick (No. 61) in the 2021 NHL Draft, was like a sponge around the team and learned everything he could in a short time. “I think it was great to get out there and play in a game and just be around that team,” he said. “I got to know a lot of guys like (veteran defensemen) Brad Hunt, Keaton Middleton, they’re really good to learn from, really good at detail guys. So just being there, being around those guys was something I really appreciated and learned a lot from, especially those two guys.” One of the most important lessons the 5-foot-11, 180-pound player from Barrington, Illinois, has learned from his professional career and focused on in the offseason is the importance of good footwork. “I think it’s just having quiet feet,” he said. “Not crossing, not falling over yourself, because guys are coming out of the quarters a little quicker. They’re finding plays a little quicker. “So being able to get close to guys, I think it’s something I’ve been working on my three years at DU and it’s gotten better, but especially at the pro level, it’s got to be really good. And if you want to be successful at the pro level, you’ve got to be able to take control of your D-zone and be successful in your D-zone.” Avalanche Director of Player Development Brian Willsie wants Behrens’ next steps to showcase his ability to develop his skills through game play. “For linebackers, in my opinion, it takes (a) season,” Willsie said. “Sometimes people spend 100 games getting linebackers acclimated. So getting out there and getting experience with older, stronger players (is key). He did well at DU. He really improved in the years he was there. “The Eagles play really up-tempo. You saw them play up-tempo games here, similar to the Avs. So start playing that style and let’s watch him improve.”
